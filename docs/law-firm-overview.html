What Law Kaki does, for your firm.

A plain explanation for deciding whether to bring it in — what it does today, what it means for your lawyers, and what to know before you commit.

The problem you already know

Ara Damansara at 2pm. Mont Kiara at 4pm. Bangsar at 6pm. Three signings, three clients, and an entire afternoon lost to KL traffic for what's often twenty minutes of actual legal work each time.

Delegating to a colleague nearby already happens — informally, over a WhatsApp message to whoever's free. It works, but there's no visibility into who's actually available, no record of who covered what, and no accountability if it goes wrong. Law Kaki turns that existing habit into a structured, trackable system.

How it works

  1. Post
    A lawyer who can't make a signing posts it — venue, time, document type, indicative fee.
  2. Notify
    Every eligible lawyer on the platform gets a WhatsApp alert and sees it on their map.
  3. Confirm
    Interested lawyers put their hand up; the poster picks one and confirms. Both sides get each other's contact.
  4. Remind & complete
    Automatic reminders fire 2 hours and 30 minutes before the appointment. Afterward, the poster marks it complete and rates the lawyer who covered it.

What it means for your firm

Recaptures billable time
Less time in transit between signings, more time on higher-value work.
No more scrambling
A scheduling conflict becomes a two-minute post instead of a round of individual messages.
Visibility without micromanaging
See who's covering what without being looped into every handoff.
Nothing changes for clients
They still deal with your firm — Law Kaki is purely internal coordination between lawyers.

What to know before you commit

No payment processing. Commission shown is indicative only — settlement happens outside the platform, the way your firm already handles it.

No Bar Council verification yet. Registration is self-serve today — a lawyer verifies their phone via WhatsApp OTP and picks their firm from a list, with no credential check or approval gate in front of it. If your firm needs tighter control before piloting, tell us — that's a scoping conversation, not a rebuild.

This is a pilot-stage product. Not a mature SaaS platform yet — expect rough edges, and expect the team behind it to want your feedback.

Getting your firm started

  • Lawyers register themselves — a WhatsApp link, a phone number, and a minute to fill in name, firm, and role.
  • No setup required on the firm's end to begin using it.
  • Want firm-only visibility, or a review step before your lawyers appear on the platform? Raise it before rollout — worth deciding deliberately rather than assuming.
